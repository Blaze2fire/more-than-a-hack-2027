docs/AUDIT_RESPONSE.md

Audit and corrections

A second AI model was asked to audit the pipeline after experiment E005. It raised nine findings. Seven were correct in whole or in part, and two of those were serious. This section is the short version; the full record is in docs/AUDIT_RESPONSE.md.

serious

1. A decision was influenced by the public ground truth

The submission script printed the public 5-minute score on every run. Smart Tagging was switched off after that score was seen to be lower with tags on. The rules forbid selecting on that number. Fixed: the script no longer reads the public ground truth; a separate check prints only PASS/FAIL for alignment and H/A orientation. Tags are now excluded by a rule that needs no score (see Decisions). We have still seen the number, and the technical document will disclose it.

serious

3. The dead-ball part of the benchmark was circular

The external games have no ball during stoppages. We invented a ball path for those frames, then tuned dead-ball cues (ball outside the pitch, ball stationary) and trained the first learned model on data containing that invention. Fixed: no model may use the ball to decide dead versus alive, and the benchmark now runs three different invented stoppage pictures so any remaining dependence shows up as a spread.

correct

2. Unpublished statistics of the competition match shaped the benchmark

The loader printed how often the ball is tracked outside the pitch. That observation prompted a change to the simulation, and the log wrongly described the change as independent of any tracking file. Fixed: statistics removed, log corrected, and after finding 3 nothing depends on the idea.

correct

8. A tidy-up step turned live play into dead ball

A short possession straight after a restart was overwritten with D. Fixed, with tests.

correct

6 and 7. Team evidence leaked into the alive-or-dead decision; overlapping tags stacked

Strong team evidence also counted as "ball alive", which is wrong at a throw-in. A decoupled scoring variant was added and tested (E007). Tag weights are now capped; tags stay off.

partly

4. Our "uncontrolled touch" rule in the labels

Not a leak, but the model was being tuned to our own reading of the possession definition. The rule removes 58 of 385 possessions in game 1, 66 of 376 in game 2 and 16 of 410 in game 3. Changed: every case is scored against labels with and without the rule, and the objective is the mean.

partly

5. In-sample numbers as the headline

Held-out checks existed, but the in-sample score was the one displayed. Changed: held-out is the first column in the log and the only number quoted here.

not supported

"The circularity caused 43% dead ball on the real match"

The true dead-ball share of the match is unpublished, so there is no legitimate evidence either way. The audit also quoted a dead-ball recall figure that can only come from the public ground truth; we have not used it.

Decision for the team

Whether to tell the organisers about finding 1 before submitting. The recommendation in the repo is yes, together with the two open questions in the data contract.

RULES.md · organiser email

The constraint that shapes everything

Parameters may only be tuned on other games

The organisers' clarification email forbids training, fitting or calibrating on the ground truth or on the provided video. Scoring two variants against the public 5 minutes and keeping the better one is tuning on the ground truth. So the first thing we built was a benchmark on external matches, and every keep-or-drop decision cites that benchmark.

How the proxy H/A/D labels are derived

The organisers say the real ground truth comes mechanically from corrected tracking: dead where the tracking marks the ball dead, otherwise the owning team. We mirror that as closely as the sample data allows. No one on the team labels anything by hand.

Making clean tracking look like ours

The sample tracking is corrected and complete. Ours is automatic, with the ball missing on 41% of graded frames. Tuning on clean data would not transfer, so external.degrade() damages it first, using only figures the organisers publish.

Every score is the average over 3 games × 3 pictures = 9 cases, and each case is scored against two label variants (with and without the uncontrolled-touch rule). Does the benchmark behave like the real task?

A second check, on the competition match itself: our loader reproduces the committed baseline_raw.csv and baseline_smooth50.csv labels on 100% of graded frames, and the loader's aggregates match the published figures (40.7% ball missing, 20.5 tracked objects, 46 slots, 638 tags).

solution/model.py

Rule-based model (Option A)

The organisers' baseline decides each frame alone and then smooths. Ours scores evidence per frame and then finds the best sequence of whole possessions. Stages, in order:

  1. Load into one internal format

    Ball position, player positions and speeds, team of each track slot, all in metres, plus the graded blocks. The same Match object is built for external games and for the competition match, so every later stage is shared.

    common.py · atd.py · external.py

  2. Per-frame measurements

    Distance from the ball to the nearest home and nearest away player; ball speed (over a 6-frame span, to tame noise); mean speed of all tracked players, smoothed over 1 s; how far the ball is outside the pitch. No parameters here.

    model.features()

  3. Team-control evidence

    A score that is positive when home is more likely in control. It fades to zero when nobody is near the ball or the ball is moving fast, so a pass flying past a defender says little, and a missing ball says nothing.

    margin = dist(nearest away) − dist(nearest home) team = k_team · tanh(margin / s_margin) · exp(−(nearest / r_near)²) ← nobody close: no evidence · 1 / (1 + (ball_speed / v_gate)²) ← ball in flight: little evidence team = 0 when the ball is not tracked
    model.evidence()

  4. Dead-ball evidence

    Log-odds of dead against alive, from player movement only: players slow down during stoppages. The ball is deliberately not used here. The external games have no ball during stoppages, so any ball-based cue would be tuned against a ball track we invented (audit finding 3).

    dead = b_dead + b_slow · (v_ref − mean player speed over 1 s) + b_slow_long · (v_ref − mean player speed over 6 s)
    model.evidence()

  5. Decode with Viterbi over three states

    Each half is decoded separately. The decoder picks the sequence of H, A and D that maximises total evidence minus a cost for every switch. With no evidence (ball missing), staying put is free, so possession carries through gaps. The favoured team scores 0 and the other team −|team|, so team evidence cannot tip the alive-or-dead decision (the "decoupled" variant, E007).

    model.viterbi3() · unit-tested against brute force

  6. Tidy and write

    H/A spells shorter than min_spell frames are absorbed into a neighbouring live spell, never into dead ball. Ungraded frames get X. The second half is forced to D before its kickoff at frame 113,742 (published in the FAQ). The file is written and checked with the official validator.

    model.absorb_short() · run.py
solution/bench.py --tune

Tuning

The rule-based model has 12 parameters. They are tuned together with a 150-iteration random search on the external benchmark. The objective is mean spellF1 over the 9 cases and both label variants. The first half of the search samples the whole range; the second half samples a shrunken box around the best so far.

The number we report is held-out. The whole search is repeated three times on two games each and scored on the third. The in-sample score of the final parameters is logged beside it and never quoted alone.

solution/learned.py · solution/bench_learned.py

Learned model (Option B)

Hand-written evidence looks only at the current frame, and hand-overs are where the rule-based model loses most. The learned model looks at a window of context. It is trained only on the external games, and the same Viterbi decoder sits on top.

After the audit it is split into two models, so that the part that decides dead versus alive never sees the ball:

Dead model: is play stopped?

  • 23 features from player movement only. No ball anywhere.
  • Mean player speed over 0.5 to 12 s, before and after; spread of speeds; fastest player; share of slow and fast players
  • Team shape: spread, gap between the two centroids, how fast the block is moving
  • Symmetric in the two teams

Team model: given live play, who has it?

  • 74 features, trained on live frames only, where the ball track is real (thinned and jittered, not invented)
  • Now: distance margin, nearest and second-nearest players per team, ball speed and position
  • Before and after, over 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 4 s: share of frames each team controlled the ball, mean margin
  • Time since and until each team last controlled the ball
  • Each match used in both team orientations, so the model cannot prefer "home"
P(D) = dead model P(H) = (1 − P(D)) · team model P(A) = (1 − P(D)) · (1 − team model) → log-probabilities → Viterbi decoder

Both are gradient-boosted trees (scikit-learn HistGradientBoostingClassifier, 300 rounds, fixed seed), trained on every 4th graded frame of each game × stoppage picture. Benchmarked leave-one-game-out for the models and for the decoder settings.

Competition-match runs

These runs use frozen parameters and models. We look only at whether the file is well-formed and plausible, in aggregate. No per-frame data from the match is printed, and no score against the public ground truth is shown.

What the alignment check does

python -m solution.sanity <file> compares the file with the public 5 minutes and prints PASS or FAIL for two questions: is it better than the same file with H and A swapped, and better than the same file shifted by two seconds? It prints no score, so it cannot be used to prefer one variant over another. Running the learned model twice gives byte-identical files.

Unverified: the dead-ball share looks high

Both files call dead ball on 44–49% of graded frames; the external games have 32–41%. The true share for this match is unpublished, and we do not measure statistics of the match to investigate. One possible cause is that the speed values in the competition tracking are distributed differently from the speeds we derive for the external games. This is an open risk, not a known fault.

Files live in evaluation/results/ (gitignored). The final file will be renamed <team_name>.csv.

Why things are the way they are

Decisions

chosen

Build an external benchmark before any model

The rules allow tuning only on other games. Without a benchmark there is no legal way to set a single threshold.

chosen

Metrica sample games as the external data

Same provider and coordinate convention as the competition tracking, events included, freely available. Three matches, about 287 minutes. Other open sets (SkillCorner) were considered but not needed yet.

chosen

Dead ball in proxy labels = ball missing in corrected tracking

In the sample data the ball is absent for 32–41% of each match, which is the stoppage time. This mirrors the organisers' own description of how their ground truth is made.

chosen

Score against labels with and without the uncontrolled-touch rule

From CHALLENGE.md: a touch without control is not a change of possession. Merging sub-2-second touches without a pass is our reading of that, so the objective averages over labels with and without the rule.

chosen

No ball-based cue for dead versus alive

The external games have no ball during stoppages, so such a cue could only be tuned against a ball track we invented. We give up a signal that is probably useful on the real match because we cannot validate it legitimately.

chosen

Three invented stoppage pictures, averaged

Any model that depends on what we put in the stoppage frames scores differently across them. The per-picture scores are logged for every experiment.

chosen

Held-out numbers only

Every tuning run repeats the search leaving each game out. That mean is the number quoted.

chosen

Decode intervals with Viterbi, not per-frame smoothing

The ranking metric counts whole possessions. A decoder with switch costs produces intervals directly and carries state through ball gaps.

chosen

Learned model as the candidate, rule-based as fallback

0.551 against 0.432 held-out, and the learned model is the less sensitive of the two to the stoppage picture.

chosen

Decoupled scoring in the rule-based decoder

Team evidence no longer counts as evidence that the ball is alive. Within noise on score, but less dependent on the stoppage picture.

dropped

Smart Tagging

Standing rule: a component whose parameters cannot be validated on external data is not used. No external game has tags, so tags are out. This decision was first made the wrong way, after seeing a public-window score (audit finding 1); the rule replaces that reasoning and would have given the same answer before any run.

dropped

Video, broadcast tracking, homography

Video needs heavy compute, and the rules bar training on it or checking output against it. Broadcast is not frame-aligned to the tactical index. Homography is only needed for video work.

not done

Measuring statistics of the competition tracking

Might be allowed for unsupervised use, but the email does not say. Only published figures are used. Two unpublished statistics were printed early on and one influenced the benchmark (audit finding 2); they have been removed.

chosen

Force D before the second-half kickoff

The FAQ publishes that frames 110,808–113,741 are graded and all dead ball. It is the one frame-specific constant in the code.

AI-assistant boundary

  • The terms forbid uploading the provided data to AI services, and this work is done with a hosted AI coding assistant.
  • Scripts that read data/ or cache/ print aggregates only: scores, counts, percentages.
  • The assistant does not open files in data/, cache/, or per-frame CSVs.
  • This page contains aggregates only.

Open questions for the organisers

  • Is unsupervised fitting on the provided tracking allowed (for example noise or gap statistics)?
  • Is it acceptable for an AI assistant to see aggregate outputs of local runs?
  • Until answered: no to the first; the second follows the boundary on the left.

What could go wrong, and what is next

Risks and next steps

Risks

  • Proxy labels are our derivation. If the organisers' notion of control differs, benchmark gains may not transfer.
  • Dead-ball share on the match is high (44–49%) and cannot be checked within the rules.
  • Ball cues for dead ball are given up, so dead-ball detection rests on player speed alone.
  • Three external games. About 1,000 possessions; enough to rank big changes, not small ones.
  • The tagging episode (audit finding 1) has to be disclosed.
  • Team size. Teams need 3–5 members to be eligible for the later stages.

Next steps

  • Team decision on reporting the tagging episode to the organisers; send the two open questions.
  • Stability of the learned model across degradation seeds and training seeds.
  • Hand-overs are still the main loss (possessions ×0.74): team-model features and decoder.
  • Technical document including the audit, pinned dependencies, one reproduction command (download external data, train, predict), clean-clone check.
